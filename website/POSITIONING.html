Brand & Positioning · Voice, pillars, tests

How we sound, what we say, and what we don't.

Positioning is what differentiates us in one sentence; brand voice is the tone that keeps every marketing surface (landing page, App Store description, press copy, support replies) coherent. This document captures both — the five message pillars that should appear everywhere, the antithesis test that prevents drift, and the visual identity. If a piece of copy fails the tests on this page, rewrite it.

1The pitch, in three lengths

Use the length that fits the channel. Don't paraphrase — these are battle-tested.

7 words

Multi-room audio without the walled garden.

28 words

SuperAudio captures any Mac audio and streams it in sync to every wireless speaker in your house — AirPlay 1, Sonos, AirPlay 2, Chromecast, Bluetooth — regardless of brand.

~80 words · App Store / press

Every multi-room audio product locks you into one ecosystem: Sonos with Sonos, Apple with Apple, Bluesound with Bluesound. SuperAudio is the one that doesn't. It captures audio from any Mac app and streams it in sync to every wireless speaker on your network — your old Bowers & Wilkins, your gen-1 Sonos Playbar, your HomePod, your kitchen Chromecast — at the same time. One-time purchase, no subscription, no cloud, LAN-only.

2Voice

How we sound. Confident, plain-spoken, specific. The opposite of marketing jargon.

What we sound like

  • Direct. "Your B&W A7 plays alongside your Sonos. Same song, same time."
  • Specific. Name the actual hardware. Mention real protocols. Cite real prices.
  • Honest about limits. "Sonos has ~200ms of internal buffering you can't bypass. We expose it as an offset slider."
  • Calm. No urgency manufactured by us. The product is for people who already know what they want.
  • Technical when asked. AVS Forum / r/audiophile audiences can see right through fluff; we use the actual terms.

What we never sound like

  • Hype words. Never "revolutionary," "game-changer," "the future of audio," "next-generation."
  • "AI." We don't use AI in the product. Don't put it in the marketing.
  • Subscription cliché. "Cancel anytime" — not relevant; we don't take subscriptions.
  • FOMO / urgency. No "limited time," "act now," "while supplies last." The product will be available.
  • Apologetic. Don't downplay limits in fine print. Lead with them honestly.

3The five pillars

Every marketing surface should make at least one of these explicit. If it doesn't, it's vague.

1
Cross-ecosystem multi-room, in sync
Your AirPlay 1, your Sonos, your HomePod — playing the same song at the same time, automatically synced to your room. Nobody else does this. Sonos won't drive your B&W. Apple won't drive your Sonos. We do both.
2
Sends the original stream to AirPlay 1
AirPlay 1 carries lossless ALAC at 44.1/16. AirPlay 2 transcodes to AAC ~256 kbps. Your old AirPort-Express-era speakers are the only wireless path Apple ever shipped that's actually lossless — and we send the original ALAC stream from Apple Music Lossless, Spotify, or any audio source to them. Your 2014 B&W A5 plays Apple Music Lossless. Your $300 HomePod doesn't.
3
No walled garden
SuperAudio doesn't care which brand made your speaker. The Mac app, the iOS companion, the Hub Stick, the Hub Pro — all treat every speaker equally. The opposite of how Sonos, Bluesound, and Apple sell their hardware.
4
One-time purchase, never subscription
$19 for the Mac app. $5 for any addon. Pay once, own forever. We don't charge for cloud features because we don't have any. Roon's $150/yr is the alternative. So is Sonos asking you to buy new speakers when they obsolete the app.
5
LAN-only, no cloud, no telemetry
Discovery, audio routing, control — all on your local network. We don't collect usage data. We don't have a server. We don't have an account system. The product runs entirely between your devices, the way it should.
6
Works with what you already own
Your 2012 B&W A5 still works. Your gen-1 Sonos Playbar still works. Your $30 Chromecast Audio still works. Buying SuperAudio doesn't ask you to replace anything — it asks your existing speakers to finally play together.

Technical framing for investors, partners, DevRel — not consumer surfaces

The five pillars above are consumer framing — concrete, brand-named, user-centric. When talking to people who understand technical layers (investors, B&W/Naim/KEF partnership conversations, Anthropic DevRel for Skills marketplace, technical press), use this sharper framing instead. It describes what SuperAudio is technically, not just what it does for users.

SuperAudio is a heterogeneous real-time audio synchronization and orchestration platform — compositional stacks, not single systems.

Three-line elaboration

  • The market has fragments. Snapcast does multi-room sync but in its own ecosystem (Snapclient on every endpoint). Shairport-sync is an AirPlay receiver. AirConnect is a one-direction protocol bridge. OwnTone is a Linux media server. Each handles one slice — none combine. (See COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.html § 3 open-source matrix for the full landscape.)
  • SuperAudio is the layer above. Composes those fragments into a unified product, then adds the pieces that don't exist anywhere yet: cross-protocol sample-accurate sync via per-chunk wall-time NTP anchor, per-sink mid-stream controls, cross-protocol Mac-mic auto-calibration (shipped 2026-05-18) + iPhone-mic Room Tuning (M11 extension), and — planned (M6.5) — a guided device-onboarding Claude Skill that walks a user through onboarding any audio device (speaker, soundbar, AVR, smart-TV-as-source) in about 10 minutes, contributing the profile back to the public MIT superaudio-device-profiles repo for every other user.
  • The technical value is not the user-facing app — that's the consumer surface. The technical value is the synchronization model + device abstraction layer + latency correction system + interoperability framework underneath. Those are the parts that could one day license to OEMs, integrate into Home Assistant, ship as an SDK, or anchor a platform business if M8 launch validates the core hypothesis. (Deferred decision; see docs/DECISIONS.md 2026-05-16 "Platform boundary" entry.)
Software adapts to your gear, not hardware-per-vendor. Every walled-garden incumbent (Sonos, Apple, Google) solves heterogeneity by trying to be the whole household — buy our hardware end-to-end and we'll make it work together. SuperAudio inverts that: the software adapts to whatever audio gear you already own — Sony soundbar with B&W AP1 speakers and a Bluetooth Bose in the guest room — by onboarding each device's specific quirks and writing a per-device profile. The planned (M6.5) guided device-onboarding Skill is the universal adapter; the hardware SKUs ($19–$249, per PRICING.html) exist only for the capture / orchestration layer where software alone isn't enough.

When to use this language

Use it for

  • Investor / acquirer conversations
  • Second-tier audiophile-brand partnership outreach (B&W, Naim, KEF, McIntosh — they speak the technical layer)
  • Anthropic DevRel / Skills marketplace launch-partner pitches
  • Hacker News submissions + technical press (The Verge, Ars Technica, The Register)
  • Engineering-recruit conversations once we hire

Don't use it for

  • Anywhere consumers land — index.html, App Store / direct-sale page, ads, social posts
  • Tutorial / onboarding / support docs
  • Customer support replies
  • Anything where "five pillars" wins by being concrete and brand-specific

The five pillars and this technical framing aren't in tension — they're two surfaces of the same product. The pillars sell what users feel; the technical framing sells what builders, partners, and investors understand. Both are accurate; neither replaces the other.

4Words to use, words to avoid

The vocabulary that supports the voice.

Use these

  • Speakers (not "endpoints", "zones", "rooms")
  • Plays (not "streams"; we're allowed to say streams in tech-audience copy but consumers don't care)
  • Your AirPlay, your Sonos, your Chromecast — possessive, makes it about the user's existing setup
  • In sync (not "synchronized"; one word is the consumer word)
  • Works with (not "supports", "integrates with")
  • Cross-ecosystem (the technical term for what we do; use in tech-audience copy)
  • Whole house, every room
  • Pay once (always; reinforces the model)
  • Tuned to your room (Room Tuning addon copy)

Avoid these

  • "Powered by" — empty, marketing-speak
  • "Smart" — meaningless, especially "smart audio"
  • "Premium" — we're not premium; we're accessible
  • "Pro" — except in actual SKU names ("Hub Pro")
  • "Studio-quality" — we're consumer-grade; making this claim courts an audiophile fight we can't win and don't need
  • "HiFi" — same reason; we're not Roon, we don't position there
  • "Unlock" — implies you're locked out; the addons add new abilities, they don't unlock something we hid
  • "Compatible with" — passive; "works with" is direct
  • "For audiophiles" — narrow audience that's already served by Roon / Bluesound
  • "Cloud" — full stop. We have nothing in the cloud and that's the point

5The antithesis test

Defining ourselves by what we're not. If a piece of copy could plausibly be said by these companies, we wrote it wrong.

NOT a HomePod buyer's argument "Buy new speakers, get lossless wireless." Except AirPlay 2 isn't lossless — it transcodes to AAC ~256 kbps.
→
WE'RE THIS Keep your old AirPlay 1 speakers — the only wireless path Apple ever shipped that's actually lossless — and we'll send Apple Music Lossless to them as the original ALAC stream.
NOT a Belkin / WiiM adapter One box per speaker, $60–149 each. Buy three, and you're at $200–450 in adapters before anything plays in sync.
→
WE'RE THIS One Mac app, every speaker, one purchase. The adapters were always the workaround, not the answer.
NOT Roon We're not for the audiophile who has $700 of DAC and a calibrated room.
→
WE'RE THIS For the household with a mix of normal speakers that should just play together.
NOT a Sonos killer We don't try to replace Sonos. Sonos is great if you only own Sonos.
→
WE'RE THIS The bridge for households that own Sonos AND other brands.
NOT Apple We don't tell you to throw out your non-Apple speakers and buy HomePods.
→
WE'RE THIS Makes your existing mix work, including the HomePod you already own.
NOT Airfoil We're not "the same thing for less money."
→
WE'RE THIS Mac AND Apple TV AND Hub Pro for the TV-anchored household. With Room Tuning.
NOT a subscription We don't take recurring money for software that should be a one-time buy.
→
WE'RE THIS Pay once. Updates included. Own it.
NOT a cloud service We don't process your audio in some datacenter.
→
WE'RE THIS Your LAN. Your devices. Nothing leaves the house.

6The three-question test

Run any new marketing copy through these. If any answer is "no", rewrite.

Q 1 · Specificity
Does it name a real speaker, protocol, or price?
Specifics are credible. "Plays to your speakers" is forgettable; "plays to your 2014 B&W A5 and your gen-1 Sonos Playbar at the same time" is the product.
Q 2 · Plain language
Could your neighbor (not a tech worker) understand it on first read?
Most of our market isn't on AVS Forum. "Cross-protocol fan-out" is the engineer's term; "plays to every speaker, even if they're different brands" is the buyer's term.
Q 3 · Antithesis
Could Sonos, Apple, Bluesound, Roon, or Spotify say this same sentence about themselves?
If yes, you've described the category, not us. Push the sentence until it's something only we can credibly say.
The shorthand: specific, plain, and only ours. Three words; three reasons to rewrite a sentence.

7Visual identity

Dark-first, gradient typography for emphasis, system fonts. Cheap to execute, hard to mistake for a competitor.

Palette

Background
#0a0a0f
Card
#14141c
Primary gradient
#ff6a3d → #ffba6d
Secondary (cyan)
#67e8f9

The orange-to-amber gradient is the primary signature. It appears on the wordmark, on featured stats, on key headlines (set in italic like this). Cyan is reserved for "info" and "defense" callouts (used sparingly so it stays distinct). Reds (#f87171) for "high risk," ambers (#fbbf24) for "medium," greens (#4ade80) for "good" or "low risk" — these are the same triad we use in the risk register and competitive landscape.

Type

System font stack only: -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "SF Pro Text", "Inter", system-ui, sans-serif. Reasons: (1) zero load time, (2) feels native on macOS / iOS, (3) signals "indie product, not corporate." Headlines use heavy weights (800) with tight letter-spacing (-0.025em); body uses 400/500.

Logo / wordmark

Wordmark only for v1 — "SuperAudio" set in the primary gradient. We don't have a logo mark yet, and that's fine. A logo mark designed before the brand is fully formed dates poorly. A clean, distinctive wordmark is enough through M8 (Mac app launch). Logo mark comes in M13 (first hardware) — it'll appear on the Hub Stick case, the box, and the warranty card.

8The point of all this

Positioning as defense
Without a fixed voice, every new piece of copy drifts toward the average.

The average for indie audio products is over-promised hype, vague claims, and a tagline that could be on any of 50 other products. Avoiding that drift requires the discipline of saying "is this specific, plain, and only ours?" out loud — every blog post, every App Store description, every support reply.

The brand isn't a logo. It's the consistency of voice across surfaces, the predictability of what we will and won't promise, and the relationship that consistency builds with customers over years. Pricing decisions are positioning decisions. Feature decisions are positioning decisions. This document is the rulebook.
