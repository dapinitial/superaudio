Risk Register · Honest about what could kill this

What could go wrong, ranked.

Every product has failure modes. Pretending they don't exist is how founders get blindsided. This document enumerates the risks across legal, technical, market, and operational dimensions, scores each by likelihood and impact, names the mitigation, and — most importantly — gives a trigger-to-act: the specific event that would tell us "the risk is materializing, do the mitigation now."

1Legal & regulatory risks

Patents, App Store policy, hardware certification.

RiskLik.Imp.MitigationTrigger-to-act
L1 · Sonos sends a patent cease-and-desist over multi-room sync. Med High We don't invent sync — we use each protocol's native mechanism. Document this defense before launch. Budget $5K for FTO patent search before M13. Any legal letter from Sonos, or pattern of Sonos suing competitors who do less than us.
L2 · App Store reverses on tvOS, requires MFi for AirPlay sending from tvOS apps. Low High M9 (Apple TV companion) becomes unshippable via App Store. Fallback: companion only on Macs and Hubs; Apple TV is a receiver only. Apple developer-policy update touching tvOS audio APIs, or rejection of M9 build.
L3 · FCC / CE / IC certification delays for Hub Stick or Hub Pro. Med Med Budget 8 weeks slack in M13/M14 timelines. Use chipset-leveraged cert where possible (Pi's CM4 has pre-cert; we ride on it). Cert lab returns first findings >3 weeks past quote.
L4 · DMCA Section 1201 challenge on AP1 RSA handshake or HDMI ARC capture. Low Med AP1 key is published numerical constant (16+ years, no enforcement). HDMI ARC capture is well-trodden (consumer extractors exist legally). Posture: we don't circumvent DRM; FairPlay content stays silent through the tap, by design. DMCA takedown of any reference implementation we use (shairport-sync, libraop, etc.).
L5 · GDPR / DSA compliance issue in EU. Low Low No data collection, no cloud, no telemetry. Paddle handles EU Merchant of Record obligations. Privacy policy template; reviewed pre-launch. EU regulator inquiry (any), or material change to DSA scope affecting indie software.
L6 · Trademark conflict on "SuperAudio." Low Low USPTO search clean as of 2026-05-13 for our exact mark in our class. Budget $1–2K for filing within year 1 if launch traction warrants. C&D from a "SuperAudio" mark holder we missed.

2Technical risks

Platform changes, protocol breaks, fundamental capture limits.

RiskLik.Imp.MitigationTrigger-to-act
T1 · Apple deprecates CATapDescription or process taps in a future macOS. Low High BlackHole (Plan B) is the documented fallback — virtual audio device that captures system output without process-tap APIs. Architecture already supports swap-in (CLAUDE.md "module boundaries"). macOS release notes deprecate the API, or sample compilation breaks on a new SDK.
T2 · B&W firmware update breaks RAOP on A5 / A7. Low High Pin dev hardware to current firmware (auto-update off). B&W discontinued the line in 2017; unlikely to push more updates. Even if they do, shairport-sync community would notice and document the change within days. B&W firmware version bump, or shairport-sync issue tracker noting break.
T3 · Apple ends AP1 entirely on receiver side (e.g., AirPort Express EOL in firmware). Low High AP1 is frozen but functional in current Apple infrastructure. Apple has zero incentive to actively kill it (hardware they no longer sell). If they do: ship a shairport-sync receiver as a separate process on Hub Stick — preserves the use case. Apple security advisory affecting AirPort Express, or AP1-only third-party speaker vendor announces end-of-support.
T4 · NTP timing-channel approach fails on a non-B&W AP1 receiver model. Med Med Code path is generic (RFC-shaped NTP, not B&W-specific). Test against AirPort Express + 2–3 other AP1 receivers before M8. If a divergent model is found, add a per-receiver quirk table. Beta-tester report: RECORD hangs on a specific AP1 model.
T5 · AAC-LC encoder latency exceeds Sonos buffer tolerance (~600ms+). Low Med AVAudioConverter low-latency mode measured. If too high, fall back to MP3 192kbps (Sonos accepts; slightly worse quality, lower latency). M4 testing: Sonos lags B&W by >800ms after manual offset.
T6 · ALAC encoder integration is harder than estimated (M3 blocker). — Resolved. Low Low ✓ Resolved 2026-05-14/15. AVAudioConverter with kAudioFormatAppleLossless produces compliant compressed ALAC natively (parameters pb=40, mb=10, kb=14 match standard SDP fmtp lines). No C-interop with Apple's open-source encoder required. The "ALAC verbatim" fallback was attempted first — B&W firmware silently drops verbatim/escape frames; compressed ALAC was mandatory, not optional. See DECISIONS.md 2026-05-14 Finding 1. Closed — first audible AP1 audio shipped 2026-05-14 on both B&W A5 and A7.
T7 · Multi-sink sync drift >100 ms despite latency probes. — De-risked by M6 sync arc 2026-05-17. Low Low ✓ M6 sync + reliability arc closed 2026-05-17. Cross-AP1 first-audio measured at <1 ms regardless of per-sink handshake variance (handshake barrier in SessionState holds all AP1 sinks until every one finishes RTSP work, then releases simultaneously). Sonos within ~200 ms of AP1 via start-align defer. User-validated empirically at slider=3085 ms. The "manual offset slider" approach (original mitigation) is now augmented by: sender-side broadcaster delay (sidesteps receivers' silent NTP-clamping behavior), supervisor exit-reason enum + retry cap (kills retry storms that competed for TCP bandwidth), and audio-gap telemetry (logs ⚠ Capture gap detected: N ms for ground-truth drop detection). See docs/DECISIONS.md 2026-05-17 entry + CASE_STUDY.html validation log. Trigger reduced to: (a) ⚠ Capture gap logs >3/hour in normal use, or (b) cross-AP1 first-audio diff >30 ms on a fresh restart (regression).
T8 · "Lossless" claim broken by accidental resampling in our pipeline (audiophile forums fact-check us within 48 hours of any marketing claim). — De-risked; discipline retained. Low High ✓ Verification shipped 2026-05-15. probe/LosslessVerify proves the ALAC encode → decode round-trip is SHA-256-identical to source (verified hash 2f9f7c83…1bb47e1). Lossless Mode toggle ships in the menu bar — forces Mac default output to 44.1 kHz while any session is active. OSLog tags the active path (◆ LOSSLESS PASS-THROUGH vs SRC) so reviewers can independently verify per-session. Discipline retained: every release re-runs probe/LosslessVerify; the 30-min soak test is still pending before any public "lossless" marketing copy goes live. Trigger reduced to: (a) probe/LosslessVerify regression on any commit touching the encode path, or (b) 30-min soak surfaces audible drift / glitch that wasn't predicted by the bit-exact test.

5What's actually scary

The High × High cell of the matrix. These three risks could kill the project; everything else is recoverable.

Existential
L1 — Sonos patent suit
Sonos is the most-litigious player in this space. They sued Google over multi-room and won material claims. If they read the COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.html on this site and decide we're a real threat, we get a letter. The defense (using each protocol's native sync, not inventing our own) is plausible but unproven in court.
Mitigation budget: $5K FTO patent search before M13 (Hub hardware). Document the "router, not inventor" defense in code comments and in DECISIONS.md. Build a relationship with a patent attorney early so we have someone to call. If we get a C&D pre-M13, pause hardware and respond formally.
Existential
O1 — Solo founder burnout
Two facts that compound: (1) the project depends on the founder's full attention through M8 and beyond; (2) the M2 RECORD-debugging night was a warning sign. Solo bootstrapped audio engineering is intense.
Mitigation: MIT-clean codebase as a load-bearing decision. Architecture documented exhaustively. If burnout fires the trigger, open-source the project — the community can maintain it indefinitely, the brand survives even if the company doesn't.
Existential
M2 — Cross-ecosystem fan-out gets commoditized
If WiiM, Sonos, or (most damagingly) Apple announces cross-ecosystem fan-out as a feature, our software moat collapses. Apple has the install base to do this overnight if they choose.
Mitigation: Cross-protocol acoustic calibration shipped 2026-05-18 in the base Mac app — 3-pass Mac-mic chirp converging to sub-perceptual sync, verified on B&W A5 + A7 + Sonos Playbar gen 1. The M11 iPhone-mic Room Tuning addon extends it with whole-room frequency correction. The moat we promised exists today — no longer a "next year" claim. Hardware moat (Hub Pro HDMI ARC) is structural. Speed to M8 is the single best mitigation. Every month of unique market presence builds brand equity that survives commoditization.

7How this register stays useful

Document discipline
A risk register that isn't revisited is just journaling.

Quarterly revisit cadence (Jan 15 / Apr 15 / Jul 15 / Oct 15). Every revisit: walk each row, ask "is the likelihood still right? has the impact changed? has the trigger fired?" Update or remove. Add any new risks discovered in the prior quarter.

The point isn't to predict the future. It's to remove "we never thought about that" from the list of possible failure stories. When the surprise comes, the response is faster because the muscle memory is in place. Risk registers are insurance; the premium is one afternoon per quarter.
