Pricing
Pricing Derivation · Defensible numbers, not guesses

Every price has a reason. Here are the reasons.

$19 for the Mac app. $5 for any addon. $59 for the Hub Stick. $79 for the Optical Hub. $249 for the Hub Pro. These numbers sit on top of three principles: impulse-purchase floors, below-Sonos and below-WiiM where it matters, and one-time payments, never subscription. This document defends each price against competitor anchors, BOM realities, and sensitivity ranges.

1The pricing philosophy

Three rules that constrain every number below.

Rule 1 — One-time purchases only

No subscriptions, not for the app, not for the addons, not for hardware. The audience we're targeting (working households with $50–150K income, a mix of legacy speakers, mild tech-savvy) is sick of $X/month for software that used to be a one-time purchase. Subscription is fashionable; we differentiate by not doing it. Roon charges $150/yr or $700 lifetime; Sonos's official multi-room is bundled into hardware you already overpaid for. We position against both by being the thing you buy once.

Rule 2 — Impulse-purchase floor for everything user-facing

The Mac app at $19 and every addon at $5 sit below the "have to think about it" threshold for the target audience. Research on app pricing consistently shows a sharp drop at $10 and another at $25–30. We straddle the $20 line for the base, and put addons firmly in the impulse-buy band. Total spend for a fully-loaded software setup (base + 3 addons) = $34. That's less than dinner for two.

Rule 3 — Anchor below the obvious comparison, beat the less-obvious one on capability

For each SKU, there's a "what would I buy instead" anchor. Mac app vs Airfoil ($35) — we're at $19, half the price. Hub Stick vs WiiM Pro Plus ($149) — we're at $59, 40%. Hub Pro vs Bluesound Node ($599) — we're at $249, 42%. The deliberate gap signals "the affordable choice" without signaling "the cheap choice." Quality positioning, not budget positioning.

Pricing is the moat. If we charged Airfoil's $35 we'd be Airfoil-with-fewer-protocols. If we charged $9 we'd signal "low quality" and undermine the premium-but-affordable position. $19 is the only number that says "real software, accessibly priced."

The derivation

  • Airfoil is the anchor. $35 since ~2008. Has every protocol, polished UI, 17 years of trust. Doing exactly what we're doing.
  • We deliberately undercut Airfoil by ~45%. Not because we offer less — we offer more on the addon path — but because we're new and the conversion from "another Mac audio app" to "the one I should buy" needs price as a forcing function.
  • $19 is above AirParrot's $16 and TuneBlade's $20. Both are narrower products (AirParrot is mirroring-first, TuneBlade is Windows-only). We position as "more than them but cheaper than Airfoil."
  • $19 is psychologically "one bill" money. A user thinking "is this $19 worth it" makes the decision in seconds. The same user thinking "is this $29 worth it" pauses, comparison-shops, and walks. Conversion-rate gap between $19 and $29 is ~2× in app-pricing literature.

What's included in $19

The base license gives the buyer:

  • Mac menu bar app with system audio capture
  • AirPlay 1 sender (RAOP) — drives every AirPort-Express-derived speaker built since 2008
  • Sonos sender (UPnP) — drives every Sonos device ever made
  • Multi-room sync within those two protocols, sample-accurate across AirPlay 1
  • Cross-protocol Mac-mic auto-calibration (shipped 2026-05-18) — built-in mic measures each speaker's arrival lag at your listening spot, aligns AirPlay to match Sonos automatically. Click Play All, hear sync — no manual tuning, no calibration mic, no UI
  • iOS companion app (free, App Store)
  • All future updates to the base app, perpetual

What's not included: AirPlay 2, Chromecast, Bluetooth fan-out, iPhone-mic Room Tuning (whole-room frequency correction), multi-zone groups. Each is a $5 a-la-carte addon. Most buyers will pick 0–2. Per-speaker delay calibration is in the base $19 — the addon is the iPhone-mic walk-around for frequency correction across multiple listening positions.

3Every addon is $5

A flat price that does three jobs at once.

Why flat

The pricing for individual addons (AirPlay 2, Chromecast, Bluetooth, Room Tuning, Multi-zone) is uniformly $5. Three reasons:

  1. Friction floor. $5 is below the threshold where users compare addons. $7 vs $9 vs $4.99 invites analysis paralysis. $5 across the board removes that cognitive load.
  2. Cumulative cap. A buyer who wants every addon spends $19 + $5×5 = $44. That's still well below Airfoil's $35-and-it-doesn't-do-Room-Tuning baseline. The all-in price stays in budget territory.
  3. Sticker effect. "Just $5" reads as inconsequential. The buyer doesn't think "do I need AirPlay 2 enough to pay $9?" They think "yeah why not, it's $5."

Why there's no "Apple TV companion" SKU

An earlier plan listed a $5 tvOS companion that would "fan out from your Apple TV instead of your Mac." We dropped it: it isn't buildable. tvOS has no system-audio capture API, forbids apps from capturing other apps' audio, and locks down DRM/FairPlay streams (Netflix, Apple TV+, Disney+). A tvOS app could only re-broadcast audio it plays itself — useless for the streaming people actually watch. The "TV → every speaker" job is done by hardware on the TV's audio output instead — the Hub Pro (HDMI ARC) and Optical Hub, below — which capture the real audio signal after the sandbox and DRM. (For all-AirPlay-2 homes, an Apple TV already fans its own audio to every AP2 speaker natively, so a third-party app would add nothing anyway.)

The iOS companion is free

Remote control and Room Tuning input. Charging for it would be hostile — the customer already paid for the base app. The Room Tuning addon is what we charge for; the iOS app that uses the addon is free. Per-speaker delay calibration is already in the base $19 via the Mac's built-in mic (shipped 2026-05-18); the addon adds the iPhone-mic walk-around for whole-room frequency correction.

Hub Pro (HDMI ARC) (in development)

Sits between TV and soundbar via HDMI ARC. Captures all TV audio (Netflix, broadcast, every smart-TV app) and fans out to wireless speakers. The headline "every TV → every speaker" product — and the only product anywhere that sends your TV's audio to your old AirPlay 1 speakers as the original ALAC stream (lossless over AirPlay 1).

Channel cost: $37.35

Anchor: Bluesound Node at $599. We're 42% of their price for a strict superset of capability (they're BluOS-only; we're cross-protocol AND we send the original ALAC stream to AP1, which they can't).
Sound-quality justification: HDMI ARC carries PCM 2.0 up to 192 kHz / 24-bit. Hub Pro encodes to ALAC (lossless compression) and sends it to the AP1 receiver over AirPlay 1. No other consumer product offers this path.

Why margins matter at these prices

Hardware margins of 37–41% are healthy for a low-volume, direct-sale electronics product. They cover: 4–8 week certification delays, warranty replacements (industry standard ~2% return rate), and the inevitable BOM creep when an ODM swap happens. For a venture-funded competitor, these margins would be unviable. For us — bootstrapped, no investor pressure to scale beyond what the founder can support — they're plenty.

5Bundling math

What "good deal" looks like — and what bundles we offer.

We deliberately do NOT bundle aggressively. Bundles are a tax on the customer's attention ("which one should I pick?") and a tax on our marketing copy ("which one are we featuring?"). The default is à la carte. Three bundles, no more:

BundleContentsSum of partsBundle priceSavings
Mac Starter Mac app + 1 addon of choice $24 $22 $2 (~8%)
Whole House Mac app + Room Tuning + AP2 + Multi-zone + Bluetooth $39 $35 $4 (~10%)
Couch Potato Hub Pro + Mac app + Room Tuning $273 $259 $14 (~5%)

The savings are modest by design. We're not Steam-sale-discounting; we're saying "if you know you want all this stuff, here's a small thank-you for committing up front." The Mac Starter bundle is the most popular projected configuration. The Couch Potato bundle is for the TV-anchored household which we believe is the largest market segment.

6International pricing

USD-anchored. Paddle handles VAT. No regional gouging.

Many indie shops region-price aggressively — €19 + 19% VAT becomes €22.61, then they round up to €24, an effective 25% markup on European buyers. We don't do this. Paddle (our Merchant of Record) computes VAT on top of the USD price at checkout. A €19 base displays as "€18 + €3.42 VAT = €21.42" to a German buyer. Honest, transparent, no surprise.

Currency display strategy

  • Default display: USD with a note "VAT/GST added at checkout for your country."
  • Geo-detected display: if the visitor is in EU/UK/AU/CA/JP, show their local currency at the current FX rate, computed from the USD anchor. Refresh weekly.
  • Never: charge regional premium pricing. The Bluesound Node is $599 in the US, £549 in the UK, €699 in Germany — that's 17% gouging on European customers. We don't.

7What we will never sell

Pricing decisions are also product decisions. These are the lines.

  • A subscription tier. Not for the app, not for cloud features (we don't have any), not for "Pro" features.
  • A free trial that becomes a charge. Either it's a one-time purchase or a free download. No surprise billing.
  • Ad-supported "free" tier. Privacy stance is non-negotiable.
  • "Hardware unlocked by software fee." If you buy a Hub Stick, every protocol the hardware can drive is unlocked. The $5 addons gate features (Room Tuning, AP2), not hardware capabilities.
  • Cross-grades or sidegrades. The Mac app and the Hub Stick are different products. We don't trade one for the other.
  • "Family" or "household" licensing complexity. One purchase = one household, defined loosely. We don't run an audit team.
  • Affiliate deals or referral commissions. Direct customer relationship; no middleman incentives.
Pricing is the easiest place to compromise the product. The harder we hold these lines, the more durable the brand.

8Strategic takeaway

Pricing as positioning
$19 says "real but accessible." $5 says "why not." $249 says "premium without paying tax."

Every number above is defensible against a specific competitor anchor. We're not the cheapest, and we shouldn't be. We're not the premium choice, and we shouldn't be. We sit in the gap between "free OSS that needs CLI" and "subscription audiophile lock-in," and we charge prices that say "this is for people who want it to just work, in their actual house, without becoming an expert."

The total spend for a fully-equipped household (Mac app + 3 addons + iOS companion + Hub Pro + Hub Stick + Room Tuning) is ~$350. The Sonos-or-Bluesound equivalent is $1,200–2,500. That ratio is the moat.
